# Book or chapter plot

Adapt these prompts to nonfiction, narrative, or a mixed work. Preserve the
choices that govern the reader's experience and omit prompts that add no
decision.

## Reader footing and promise

What situation, question, desire, or conflict can the reader enter? What lasting
understanding, experience, or transformation does the work promise, and why is
that valuable to this reader?

## Arc

Describe the entry, development, turns, pace, emphasis, revelation, echoes, and
ending. Map content-model meanings or narrative changes into that arc according
to the experience: one may span several passages, and several may combine in
one movement.

## Nonfiction development

- What argument or explanatory journey controls the work?
- Which meanings carry the promise, and what grounds them?
- Where do examples create understanding?
- Which concepts deserve sustained development?
- Where do objections, alternatives, and limitations change the path?

## Narrative development

- What desire, conflict, causality, or question carries the movement?
- What changes in knowledge, stakes, relation, or agency?
- What work does each scene or episode perform here?
- Whose viewpoint governs what can be revealed?

## Chapter and passage movement

Use cards, prose, or diagrams where comparison or rearrangement helps. Capture
the inherited footing, internal development, turn, exit state, bridge, source
material, and unresolved problem that matter for each major movement. Give a
previously troubled continuous passage enough detail to guide its internal arc.

## Rhythm and proportion

Mark where the work compresses, expands, pauses, accelerates, recalls, or lands.
Let importance and reader experience govern space.

## Revision note

State what changed in this revision and why.
