# Document-kind guidance

Read the section matching the document kind when its conventions affect content
design, plotting, or acceptance.

## Technical documents

Begin with the reader's work or learning situation, prior knowledge, constraints,
and useful outcome. Choose the primary form from that use:

- a tutorial creates a guided learning experience;
- a how-to guide supports completion of a real task;
- reference supports accurate lookup during work;
- explanation builds understanding of causes, relationships, or operation; and
- a decision or design document establishes a choice, its reasons, constraints,
  alternatives, and consequences.

A document may link to other forms while each continuous passage retains a clear
purpose.

Primary guidance:

- Google Technical Writing: [Audience](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/audience), [Documents](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/documents), and [Organizing large documents](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/two/large-docs)
- Diátaxis: [overview](https://diataxis.fr/start-here/) and [workflow guidance](https://www.diataxis.fr/how-to-use-diataxis/)

## Academic documents

Connect the target scholarly audience's research situation to the work's
contribution and value. Model the claims, warrants, evidence, methods,
qualifications, and implications that establish that contribution. Let the study
or article type, venue, and reporting requirements constrain its presentation.

Primary guidance:

- ICMJE, [Preparing a Manuscript for Submission](https://icmje.org/recommendations/browse/manuscript-preparation/preparing-for-submission.html)
- EQUATOR Network, [Reporting guidelines](https://www.equator-network.org/)

## Books and chapters

Connect a situation, question, desire, or conflict the reader can enter to the
work's lasting promise. Give the whole work and each chapter or major movement
an arc that contributes to that promise.

For nonfiction, track argument, explanation, examples, and echoes across the
work. For narrative writing, track causality, viewpoint, revelation, stakes,
and change. A chapter provides its own movement and changes the reader in a way
the work-level promise requires.
