# Meaning-tree procedure

Apply this procedure when a new document or substantial revision needs its
meaning established before prose is composed. A meaning tree is a recursive
account of what the document establishes. It is not an outline of headings and
does not prescribe one sentence or paragraph per node.

The meaning tree is the document's governing logical structure. Design it top
down from the root and verify it bottom up from the leaves. Each node states a
meaning; its children show what establishes, develops, qualifies, or applies
that meaning. A document is logically complete only when its material leaves
connect through explicit contribution relations to the root and those relations
can be recovered from the finished prose.

## Establish a reader-valued root

Begin with the intended reader, not the source material's organization.
Distinguish two related decisions:

- the **reader outcome** is what reading enables the intended reader to
  understand, judge, decide, or do; and
- the **root** is the substantive governing meaning the document establishes.

The root must matter to the reader, but it is not a meta-description of reading
the document. “The designer can understand what the component records” may be a
reader outcome; it does not state the product or design proposition the reader
must evaluate.

The root may name the document's answer or method, but a product, component, or
topic is not by itself a root. For a product-positioning or design document,
express the end-user or organizational value created by the product before the
internal mechanism that enables it. A statement that the document lets its
technical reader judge scope, records, or conditions belongs in the brief, not
at the root of the product's meaning tree.

Test the root by asking:

- What becomes possible, safer, clearer, or decidable for this reader?
- What problem, task, question, or situation makes that change valuable?
- Would the root still mean something to the reader before they know the
  document's internal terminology?

## Develop the semantic spine with the author

Derive the major meanings the reader must understand or accept for the root to
hold. For each group of children, state how they jointly establish the parent.
Use relations such as cause, dependency, evidence, mechanism, contrast,
qualification, sequence, or consequence only when they describe the actual
meanings involved.

Collaborate most near the root. A node needs author resolution when a plausible
alternative would change:

- the reader value or governing conclusion;
- a major supporting branch or its priority;
- the strength, scope, or qualification of a central claim;
- a product, policy, design, or interpretive choice owned by the author; or
- the meaning of a central domain concept.

The relevant depth varies by branch. A high-impact or uncertain leaf may need
author resolution even when nearby branches do not. Conversely, a large amount
of source-determined detail can be expanded without author review.

Build shared understanding through ordinary conversation. The author may state
a node, correct a proposed relation, extend a branch, or respond to a synthesis.
Collaboration depth follows semantic impact and uncertainty rather than a fixed
tree level.

Before composition, the agent states its own coherent understanding of the
current tree from the root through the depth needed to constrain the document.
This synthesis makes the major meanings and their load-bearing relations,
conditions, and qualifications visible. The author then explicitly confirms or
corrects it. Continue this exchange until the current synthesis is confirmed.

The mutual confirmation is mandatory because it demonstrates both directions:
the agent has interpreted the author's intent, and the author has seen what the
agent will use as the governing meaning. A general instruction to write, the
agent's private confidence, source consistency, an existing draft, or an
agent-authored artifact cannot substitute for it.

The method of reaching confirmation remains flexible. Do not require a formal
meeting, special phrase, fixed representation, approval of each node, or review
of the complete lower tree. Once confirmed nodes constrain the document, mark
them as the semantic spine and stop requesting intermediate approval unless a
later discovery would change them.

## Expand downward

Develop each branch to the depth at which a writer can create a coherent
passage without inventing a relationship or recovering hidden author intent.
Possible children include claims, reasons, mechanisms, distinctions, evidence,
examples, definitions, conditions, exceptions, actions, or consequences.

For each developed node, make recoverable:

- the proposition or meaning;
- its contribution to its parent;
- how its children combine to establish it;
- its epistemic status, scope, conditions, and qualifications;
- its support and provenance where relevant; and
- its important dependencies on terms, examples, or earlier meanings.

Use prose, a tree, a diagram, or a mixture. Do not impose a uniform record on
every node. Add detail only where it changes reasoning, evidence, or writing.

## Carry terminology and examples

Keep one primary name for each concept. Record enough to preserve:

- its meaning and boundary in this document;
- nearby concepts that must remain distinct;
- an original, standard, or code name needed for traceability; and
- any wording that is prohibited because it changes the domain meaning.

Treat examples as semantic evidence, not decoration. For a material example,
identify its inputs, conditions, operation or mechanism, result, and the exact
proposition it supports. Calculate or execute it when possible. Label a
contrived example and do not present mere possibility as a representative case.

## Use an existing draft as material

Reverse-outline the existing draft to recover its claims, explanations,
examples, evidence, gaps, and reader effects. Design the target tree from the
reader-valued root rather than copying the draft's headings. Derive each target
branch from its parent before mapping existing material into that branch.

The comparison reveals useful material, missing support, misplaced detail,
unsupported claims, and passages whose current prominence does not match their
importance to the root.

## Verify upward

Read developed branches from their leaves toward the root. At each parent ask:

- Are the children necessary or materially useful?
- Do they jointly establish the parent at its stated strength?
- Is support attached to the claim it actually warrants?
- Do conditions and exceptions have the correct scope?
- Does an example demonstrate the claimed mechanism or only resemble it?
- Did any branch depend on source headings or unstated author knowledge?

Repair source-determined gaps directly. Surface a concise semantic fork when
resolution requires the author. A complete tree is ready for composition when
the difficult branches can guide connected passages and upward verification
reaches the shared root without invented support.

## Preserve authority during composition

The semantic spine binds every draft. The lower tree remains a working semantic
model: writing may expose a missing relation or a better source-supported
decomposition. Update prose and the lower tree together, but do not silently
change an author-owned node.

After drafting, a fresh reviewer reconstructs a tree from the document alone.
That recovered tree is evidence about what the prose communicates; it does not
replace the intended tree. Compare the two and route each difference to prose,
the lower tree, sources, or the author according to who owns it.

Presence is not structural fidelity. Compare the recovered hierarchy,
contribution relations, qualifications, and relative prominence, not merely
whether each intended meaning appears somewhere in the draft.

Revision below the semantic spine does not require renewed author agreement.
If a revision changes the root or another confirmed spine meaning, present the
revised synthesis and obtain mutual confirmation again before composing from
it.
